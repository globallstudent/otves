## 1. Uzbekistan's norm system: ShNK vs KMK, what replaced what, use of Russian SP/GOST, where texts are published

### Takeaway
The binding Uzbek seismic code is **KMK 2.01.03-19 "Строительство в сейсмических районах"**. It was approved by Minstroy order No. 561 of 23.12.2019, has been in force since **1 March 2020**, and **replaced KMK 2.01.03-96**. Uzbekistan runs two series of norms side by side. **KMK (ҚМҚ)** are construction norms and rules. **ShNK (ШНҚ)** are urban-planning norms and rules, and newer documents also use this label. Interstate **GOST** standards are part of the national base. Russian SP documents are not the binding seismic norm. A Cabinet resolution of November 2025 (No. 727) ordered further changes to the 2.01.03 norms by April 2026. I could not confirm whether those changes were published.

### Inferences
- In the app, label the norm as **"KMK 2.01.03-19 (ҚМҚ 2.01.03-19)"** and keep a version field. Resolution 727 ordered changes to 2.01.03 by April 2026, so an amendment or a re-issue (possibly under a ShNK label) may now exist.
- Russian SP 14.13330 is a close relative: both descend from SNiP II-7-81. Many clauses are word-for-word the same (belts, meshes, lintels). Treat SP as a **reference or fallback only**. In Uzbekistan the KMK numbers prevail where they differ.
- Interstate GOSTs (brick, rebar, concrete) are used in Uzbekistan, so material grades in the app can follow GOST notation: M75/M100 bricks, B12.5/B15 concrete, A240/A400 rebar.

## 2. Seismic intensity (MSK-64) of Uzbek cities and regions: current map/list

### Takeaway
Two official lists apply, and they conflict. **KMK 2.01.03-19, Appendix 1 (mandatory)** lists 359 settlements with recurrence periods for 7/8/9/9\* points on average (category II) soil. **CM Resolution No. 727 (17.11.2025), Annex 2** lists settlements with **≥8 points** and gives higher values for many cities: **Andijan 9; Tashkent 8–9 (Uchtepa and Shayxontohur districts 9); Namangan region mostly 9; Bukhara 8–9; Fergana 8–9; Samarkand, Jizzakh, Navoi, Gulistan and Karshi 8; Termez 7–8.** Nukus is **6** in KMK Appendix 1. Final site intensity is then adjusted for soil category (KMK Table 1.1) or taken from microzoning (СМР) maps.

### Cited Findings
- **KMK cl. 1.5–1.6.** Intensity and recurrence come from mandatory Appendices 1 (list) and 2 (general seismic zoning map) and refer to **category II soils**. Site seismicity should come from seismic microzoning (СМР). Without СМР maps it is set from Appendices 1–2 plus Table 1.1. In ВОЗ-zone settlements without СМР maps, **construction is not allowed** without extra studies — [KMK 2.01.03-19](https://eis.su/srostandart/kmk-2-01-03-19/)
- **KMK Table 1.1 (soil adjustment).** For region intensity 7 / 8 / 9:
  - Category I (rock, Vs > 1700 m/s etc.) → 6 / 7 / 8.
  - Category II → 7 / 8 / 9.
  - Category III → 8 / 9 / >9.
  - Category III includes **loess soils (лессы, лессовидные суглинки) with e ≥ 0.8 or IL > 0.5 or Vs ≤ 300 m/s**, clays with IL > 0.5, and loose or water-saturated sands.
  - If the top 10 m layer is non-uniform, the less favourable category applies.

### Inferences
- **The app's settlement → intensity lookup should use the newest list.** That is Resolution 727 Annex 2, or the Online Seismological Platform if it is accessible. Fall back to KMK Appendix 1 for settlements below 8. Where a range is given (8–9), require the user to pick a district or zone, or **default to the higher value**.
- Resolution 727 values are about +1 point higher than KMK Appendix 1 for several cities: Bukhara 7→8–9, Jizzakh/Navoi/Karshi/Gulistan 7→8, Termez 7→7–8, Namangan 8→9 in most of the region. This is consistent with the OSR-2017 maps superseding the older list.
- **Soil category is a decisive input.** Much of Uzbekistan's urban land sits on loess. Loess with e ≥ 0.8 or IL > 0.5 is category III, which adds +1 point: an 8-point city becomes 9, and a 9-point site becomes ">9". **App use:** ask for soil category from the geotechnical report. If it is unknown, default to category III (conservative) and show a warning.
- **App use, worked out:** design intensity = f(settlement intensity, soil category) per KMK Table 1.1, or a microzoning value if the user enters one. The result drives every rule in sections 3–4.

## 3. Masonry house rules by seismicity in KMK 2.01.03-19 (7 / 8 / 9 points), with app use

### Takeaway
The KMK sets hard limits on storeys, wall spacing and openings, which depend on **masonry category**. Category is defined by the **measured normal bond Rtu**: **"high" (высшая) ≥ 500 kPa; I ≥ 180 kPa; II 120–180 kPa; below 120 kPa not allowed**. For example, **plain category-II brick masonry allows only 1 storey at 8–9 points**. The KMK also prescribes the elements the app can auto-add:
- belts at every floor and roof level, ≥150 mm high, concrete ≥ B12.5, **4Ø10 at 7–8 / 4Ø12 at 9 / 4Ø14 at >9 and 9\***;
- junction meshes ≥ 1 cm², 1.5 m long, **every 700 mm (7–8) / 500 mm (9)**;
- RC cores at pier ends and at ≤ 5 m in blank walls (complex construction);
- lintels with **≥ 350 mm bearing (≥ 250 mm for openings ≤ 1.5 m)**;
- a reinforced mortar layer on precast strip foundations, **3/4/6 Ø10**.

### Cited Findings
Unless stated otherwise, every item below is from [KMK 2.01.03-19 (full text)](https://eis.su/srostandart/kmk-2-01-03-19/).

#### 3.1 General layout (KMK cl. 3.1.1, 3.1.3)
- The building should have a regular plan. Projections should normally be **≤ 2 m for masonry/brick buildings**. Height steps within one compartment should be ≤ 6 m. Different structural systems in one building are not allowed without seismic joints. **App use:** flag plan projections > 2 m; suggest a seismic joint.
- **Seismic joints** must split the building over its full height; the foundation may stay continuous unless the joint coincides with a settlement joint. The width text gives 30 mm, increased by 20 mm per 5 m of height (cl. 3.1.3 area). **App use:** for an L/T-shaped or long house, propose a joint as paired walls with a gap of ≥ 30 mm.

#### 3.2 Table 3.1: height, storeys, spans, transverse-wall spacing, building length (masonry rows)
Rows as read from the code (format: height m (storeys); intensity 7 / 8 / 9 / >9 / 9\*):
- **Row 4: complex construction** (masonry with monolithic RC inclusions), hand-laid:
  - **high and I category:** height **30(7) / 26(6) / 22(5) / 12(3) / 7(2)**. Span and transverse-wall step: "ТНСР" (i.e. per non-seismic rules) at 7–9; 7.2 m at >9; 6 m at 9\*. Max length of building/compartment 80 / 60 / 40 / 30 / 24 m.
  - **II category:** height **26(6) / 22(5) / 18(4) / 9(2) / 5(1)**. Span and wall step ТНСР at 7–9; 6 at >9; 4.8 at 9\*. Length 60 / 40 / 30 / 24 / 20 m.
- **Row 5: walls of ceramic or concrete stones or brick, hand-laid** (plain, not complex):
  - **high and I category:** height **22(5) / 18(4) / 12(3)** at 7 / 8 / 9; not allowed at >9 and 9\*. **Span 12 / 9 / 7.2 m.** **Transverse-wall step 15 / 12 / 9 m.** **Length 40 / 30 / 20 m.**
  - **II category:** height **9(2) / 5(1) / 4.5(1)**. **Span 12 / 9 / 6 m.** **Transverse-wall step 12 / 9 / 7.2 m.** **Length 30 / 24 / 18 m.**
- **Row 6: low-strength materials** (adobe/сырцовый кирпич, earth blocks, грунтобетон):
  - (a) with seismic measures and a stone foundation/plinth: **4.5(1) at 7 points only**; span 5 m; wall step 5 m; length 20 m.
  - (b) with a timber frame: **8(2) at 7, 4(1) at 8**, not allowed at 9; span 6 / 6; step 6 / 6; length 24 / 18 m.
  - (c) with RC elements: **8(2) / 7(2) / 5(1)** at 7 / 8 / 9; span 9 / 7 / 5; step 6 / 5 / 4; length 24 / 18 / 12 m.
- **Notes to Table 3.1:**
  - Height is measured from the lowest blind-area/ground level to the **top of the external walls**.
  - Hospitals and schools are limited to 3 storeys (kindergartens to 2) at 8–9 points.
  - **The storey count governs** when height and storeys conflict (note 9).
  - A storey counts as above-ground if more than half its height is above grade; a technical floor > 2.4 m also counts (note 7).
  - Deviations require Special Technical Conditions (СТУ).
- **Cl. 3.5.7:** the distance between transverse-wall axes must be checked by calculation and must not exceed Table 3.1.
- **App use:**
  - Inputs: wall system (plain / complex with cores / adobe), masonry category, intensity.
  - Checks: number of storeys, building height, longest clear span, max distance between transverse walls (scan the plan for wall axes), compartment length.
  - Auto-fix suggestion: "add RC cores → complex construction" when plain masonry fails the storey limit.

#### 3.3 Materials (KMK cl. 3.5.1–3.5.3)
- **Bond:** single-row (chain) bond. Multi-row bond is allowed only at 7 points, with header courses at least every 3 stretcher courses.
- **Not allowed** in load-bearing or self-bearing walls: lightweight masonry with internal insulation layers (cavity walls with insulation inside).
- Mortar should contain plasticisers and special adhesion-increasing additives.
- **At > 9 points** hand-laid walls must be strengthened by one of these:
  - horizontal reinforcement plus RC inclusions;
  - the same plus enhanced meshes at wall junctions;
  - a vertical one- or two-sided reinforced mortar/concrete layer.
- **At 9\*:** enhanced horizontal reinforcement plus RC inclusions plus enhanced junction meshes, *or* a two-sided reinforced layer.
- **Winter masonry** is banned at ≥ 9 points. Below 9 it is allowed only with antifreeze additives (cl. 3.5.2).
- **Units allowed (cl. 3.5.3):**
  - (a) **fired solid or hollow brick, grade ≥ M75**, holes ≤ 14 mm, voids ≤ 20%;
  - (b) **ceramic stones ≥ M100**, holes ≤ 14 mm, voids ≤ 20%;
  - (c) **concrete stones and solid/hollow blocks** (including lightweight concrete ≥ 1200 kg/m³) **≥ M50**;
  - (d) at 7 points only: ceramic stones ≥ M75 with slots ≤ 12 mm or holes ≤ 14 mm, voids ≤ 20%.
- **Mortar:** mixed cement mortar **≥ M50**.
- **Prohibited outright:**
  - fired brick or ceramic stone with **horizontal voids**;
  - **silicate (sand-lime) brick** ("Применение силикатного кирпича в сейсмических районах запрещается").
- Natural-stone blocks, cellular-concrete blocks below **B3.5** and unfired bricks may be used only under separate normative documents.
- External insulation of 80–125 kg/m³ is recommended.
- **App use:**
  - Block selection of silicate brick and horizontally-perforated brick in seismic zones.
  - Validate brick grade ≥ M75 (fired brick), ceramic stone ≥ M100, concrete block ≥ M50, mortar ≥ M50.
  - Warn on aerated block below B3.5 (not covered by the KMK directly).
  - Warn on winter masonry at ≥ 9 points.

#### 3.4 Masonry category and bond strength (KMK cl. 3.5.4–3.5.5)
- The category is set by the **ultimate axial tensile strength across unbonded joints (normal adhesion) Rtu**:
  - **high category:** Rtu ≥ 500 kPa (5 kgf/cm²);
  - **category I:** 500 > Rtu ≥ 180 kPa (1.8 kgf/cm²);
  - **category II:** 180 > Rtu ≥ 120 kPa (1.2 kgf/cm²).
- The required Rtu **must be stated in the design** and based on tests in the construction region.
- If **Rtu ≥ 120 kPa cannot be achieved** (even with additives), **brick or stone masonry is not allowed**.
- **Control tests on site are mandatory.** Building load-bearing or self-bearing brick walls **without control tests is not allowed**. The contractor must provide a certificate guaranteeing the bond value.
- If tests show low bond, **work must stop**; the masonry already built is strengthened or **demolished**. After **three violations** the firm is removed from the site, and loss of licence or criminal or administrative action may follow.
- A masonry work method statement (ППР), including curing measures, is mandatory.
- **Note to 3.5.4:** at 7 points, with the state body's approval, Rtu 60–120 kPa is allowed if: **≤ 2 storeys, piers ≥ 0.9 m, openings ≤ 2 m, wall-axis spacing ≤ 12 m**.
- **Design strengths across unbonded joints:** Rt = 0.45·Rtu; Rsq = 0.7·Rtu; Rtw = 0.8·Rtu (cl. 3.5.5).
- **App use:**
  - Input: Rtu test result, or the target category. Category I is the default assumption when no test exists, but show it as "requires test".
  - The category drives Table 3.1, Table 3.2 and Table 3.3.
  - Generate a site-checklist item: "bond test (нормальное сцепление) before walls above the first storey".

#### 3.5 Storey height and slenderness (KMK cl. 3.5.6)
- **Plain masonry:** storey height **≤ 5 / 4 / 3.5 m** at 7 / 8 / 9 points. **h/t ≤ 12** for brick and artificial stone; **≤ 9** for natural stone.
- **With RC inclusions:** **≤ 6 / 5 / 4.5 m** at 7 / 8 / 9; **3.5 m at >9 and 3 m at 9\***. h/t ≤ 15 up to 9 points, ≤ 10 at >9 and 9\*.
- **Buildings with load-bearing walls and ≥ 2 storeys** must have **at least one internal longitudinal wall** in addition to the external ones.
- **App use:** check floor-to-floor height against wall thickness (e.g. a 380 mm wall gives max 4.56 m at h/t 12). Flag a missing internal longitudinal wall for 2-storey houses.

#### 3.6 Piers, openings, wall area (KMK Tables 3.2 and 3.3, cl. 3.5.8–3.5.9)
- **Table 3.2: minimum pier width (простенок), m,** at 7 / 8 / 9 / (>9, 9\*):
  - high category: **0.6 / 0.7 / 0.9 / 1.2**;
  - I category: **0.77 / 0.9 / 1.16 / 1.55**;
  - II category: **0.9 / 1.16 / 1.55 / 2.0**.
  - Piers with height/width > 2 (brick) or > 3 (complex) are ignored in the seismic calculation.
- **Maximum opening width:** **3.5 / 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.0 m**. Wider openings must be framed with RC elements.
- **Minimum pier/opening width ratio:** **0.25 / 0.35 / 0.5 / 1.0**.
- **Table 3.3: minimum total horizontal wall cross-section in each direction**, as % of the floor area measured by the external perimeter (for **category II**):
  - brick/stone masonry **3 / 4.5 / 6%** at 7 / 8 / 9 (not allowed at >9 and 9\*);
  - complex construction **2 / 3 / 4.5 / 5.5 / 6.5%** at 7 / 8 / 9 / >9 / 9\*;
  - for high and I category masonry the area may be **reduced by 50% and 25%** respectively.
- **App use:**
  - Per wall, compute piers between openings and compare with Table 3.2. Compare opening widths with the limits and auto-add an RC frame (обрамление) when an opening is too wide. Check the pier/opening ratio.
  - Per storey and direction, compute Σ(wall length × thickness) / floor area and compare with Table 3.3.

#### 3.7 Antiseismic belts (KMK cl. 3.5.10–3.5.11)
- Belts are required **at the level of every floor and roof, on all longitudinal and transverse walls**. They are monolithic RC cast after the slabs are placed, or precast with a ribbed side face and continuous reinforcement.
- Top-storey belts must be tied to the masonry with vertical rebar dowels or RC ties.
- Belts may be omitted where **monolithic RC slabs are embedded in the walls around their whole perimeter**.
- **Size:** belt (with the slab bearing zone) **normally the full wall thickness**. For external walls **≥ 500 mm thick** the belt may be **100–150 mm narrower**. **Height ≥ 150 mm.** **Concrete ≥ B12.5.**
- **Longitudinal reinforcement:** **4Ø10 at 7–8 points; ≥ 4Ø12 at 9; 4Ø14 at >9 and 9\***.
- **App use:**
  - Auto-generate a belt polyline along all bearing walls at each floor and roof level. Width = wall thickness (or t − 100…150 mm if t ≥ 500). Height = max(150 mm, user value).
  - Rebar 4Ø10 / 4Ø12 / 4Ø14 by intensity. Concrete default B15 (≥ B12.5 per code).
  - Estimate: concrete m³ = Σ wall length × width × height. Steel 4Ø10 = 2.47 kg/m; 4Ø12 = 3.55 kg/m; 4Ø14 = 4.83 kg/m (computed from ρ = 7850 kg/m³). Add stirrups as a user-set default (see Gaps).

#### 3.8 Meshes at wall junctions (KMK cl. 3.5.12)
- At wall junctions, lay meshes with **total longitudinal steel area ≥ 1 cm²**, **1.5 m long**, **every 700 mm of height at 7–8 points and every 500 mm at ≥ 9 points**.
- **Enhanced meshes** (where cl. 3.5.1 requires them): **≥ 1.5 cm² every 300 mm (>9) and every 200 mm (9\*)**, with ≥ 0.15% volumetric reinforcement of the junction zone.
- **App use:**
  - At every corner and T/X junction, auto-place mesh layers at a pitch of 700 or 500 mm. The number of courses comes from the course height input, e.g. 65 mm brick + 12 mm joint ≈ 77 mm per course, so 700 mm ≈ every 9 courses and 500 mm ≈ every 6–7 courses.
  - Check bar areas: 4Ø6 = 1.13 cm² passes; 2Ø8 = 1.01 cm² passes; a 4Ø4 wire strip = 0.50 cm² **fails**.
  - Count meshes for the estimate: junctions × floor height / pitch.

#### 3.9 Parapets, attic walls, gables, brick columns (KMK cl. 3.5.12)
- **Wall sections and columns above the attic floor higher than 400 mm** must be reinforced and strengthened with monolithic RC inclusions **anchored into the belt**. This covers parapets and gable/attic walls.
- **Brick columns** are allowed **only at 7 points**, with mortar ≥ M50 and height ≤ 4 m, tied in two directions by beams anchored into the walls.
- **Small-element roofing** (tiles, asbestos sheets): **every element must be fastened** to the structure (cl. 3.9.9).
- **App use:** if a parapet or gable is taller than 0.4 m, auto-add vertical RC cores anchored to the top belt (plus a capping beam per SP; see section 4). Block brick columns at ≥ 8 points.

#### 3.10 Vertical RC cores / complex construction (KMK cl. 3.5.13)
- **Location:** cores in complex-construction buildings must connect to the belts and be **open on at least one side**. They go **at the ends of piers (по торцам простенков)** and, in blank walls, at **≤ 5 m spacing**.
- **At wall junctions:** the masonry must be strengthened by RC inclusions **within 2 m of the intersection**.
- **Materials:** concrete **≥ B12.5**; masonry mortar **≥ M50**.
- **Alternative strengthening:** a vertical cement mortar/concrete layer **≥ 25 mm**, strength ≥ 100 kgf/cm², on mesh fixed to the wall. Ties **≥ 1 cm² per 1 m² of wall face (≥ 2 cm² at >9 and 9\*)**, spaced ≤ 50 cm.
- **App use:** in "complex" mode, auto-place cores at all corners, at T/X junctions (or within 2 m of them), at both sides of every opening (pier ends), and at ≤ 5 m in long blank walls. Connect cores to the belts in the 3D/elevation view. Input: core section (≥ 120 mm if open, ≥ 150 mm if closed per SP) and bar count.

#### 3.11 Lintels, stairs, ground floors (KMK cl. 3.5.14–3.5.17, 3.1.5, 3.1.10)
- **Lintels:** normally the full wall thickness, **bearing ≥ 350 mm**; **≥ 250 mm for openings ≤ 1.5 m** (cl. 3.5.15). **App use:** lintel length = opening + 2 × 350 mm (or + 2 × 250 mm if the opening is ≤ 1.5 m). Check that the adjacent pier is long enough for the bearing.
- **Stairs:**
  - Landing beams are embedded **≥ 250 mm** in masonry and anchored. Steps, stringers and precast flights must be fastened.
  - **Cantilever steps embedded in masonry are prohibited.**
  - Door and window openings in stairwell masonry walls at 8–9 points normally need RC framing (cl. 3.5.16).
  - In buildings over 2 storeys (6 m) at > 8 points, **stairwells must not be attached or free-standing volumes** (cl. 3.1.5).
- **Ground floors:** shops and similar large open ground floors in load-bearing-wall buildings must use an RC structure (cl. 3.5.14).
- **Floor bearing on brick walls (cl. 3.1.10):**
  - precast slabs **≥ 120 mm**;
  - timber or steel purlins **≥ 120 mm** for spans up to 6.5 m and **≥ 150 mm** for longer spans;
  - profiled deck **≥ 80 mm**.
- **Timber roof beams** must be anchored into the belt, with diagonal boarding.
- **Precast slabs** without keyed side faces or end anchor bars are **not allowed**, except as permanent formwork under a ≥ 100 mm doubly-reinforced topping (concrete ≥ B25). Slab joints are grouted with ≥ B7.5. A spaced-slab option uses a ≥ 120 mm gap with a 4Ø10 cage and Ø6 @ 200 in B15 (cl. 3.1.11).
- **App use:** check slab bearing ≥ 120 mm. Flag cantilever steps in brick. Auto-anchor landings.

#### 3.12 Foundations (KMK cl. 3.1.7–3.1.9)
- **Precast strip foundations:** on top, lay a **cement mortar M100 layer ≥ 40 mm** with longitudinal bars **3 / 4 / 6 Ø10 at 7 / 8 / 9 points**, joined by **Ø6 cross bars every 300–400 mm**.
- **At > 9 and 9\*:** strip foundations normally **monolithic, with 6Ø12 bottom bars**. Precast is allowed on a 10 cm reinforced RC slab, with an M100 top layer reinforced with 6Ø12.
- **Levels:** foundations normally at one level. Steps **≤ 60 cm high, slope ≤ 1:2**. Depth as for non-seismic areas.
- **Basement walls of large blocks:**
  - bond in every course and at corners to **≥ 1/3 block height**;
  - joint mortar **≥ M100**;
  - at 9 / >9 / 9\*: corner meshes **2 m long, 1 / 1.5 / 2.0 cm², every ≤ 1 m**, and basement belts **4Ø12 / 4Ø14 / 4Ø16**;
  - hollow blocks up to 25% voids allowed only for ≤ 3 storeys at 7–8 points;
  - **rubble-concrete (бутобетон) foundations only for ≤ 3 storeys at 7 points**.
- **Damp-proof courses** must be **cement mortar ≥ 30 mm** (cl. 3.1.9).
- **App use:**
  - Auto-add a "foundation seismic layer" element: M100 mortar 40 mm + n × Ø10 (n = 3/4/6) + Ø6 @ 300–400.
  - For monolithic strip foundations, the same bars can sit in a top belt (equivalent function; inference).
  - Block rubble-concrete at ≥ 8 points or > 3 storeys.
  - Replace a bitumen/roll DPC in the bed joint with ≥ 30 mm cement mortar.

#### 3.13 Partitions (KMK cl. 3.9.7)
- Brick partitions are reinforced along their full length **at least every 700 mm of height**, with bars totalling **≥ 0.2 cm² per joint at 7 points and ≥ 0.3 cm² at 8–9 points**.
- **App use:** auto-add bed-joint bars in partitions, e.g. 2Ø4 = 0.25 cm² (passes at 7 only) or 2Ø5 = 0.39 cm².

#### 3.14 Adobe / low-strength-material houses (KMK section 3.7), common in rural Uzbekistan
- Low-strength materials have compressive strength < 3.5 MPa: adobe (сырцовый кирпич), earth blocks, грунтобетон, and brick infill of timber or RC frames.
- **Plan and walls:**
  - Buildings wider than 5 m need ≥ 1 internal longitudinal wall; all internal walls run through.
  - **Total pier area ≥ 4% of plan area per direction.** **Piers ≥ 1.0 m. Openings ≤ 1.5 m**, framed in timber or RC cores. **h/t ≤ 9.** No plan projections.
  - The weight of upper-storey walls must not exceed that of the lower storey.
- **Bond:** Rtu ≥ 0.3 kgf/cm² (30 kPa).
- **Belts:** around all walls, timber or RC.
  - RC belt: concrete **≥ B5**, full wall width, vertical dowels anchored 30 cm at ≤ 0.5 m.
  - Timber belt: logs or 2 beams of 10 × 10 cm joined at ≤ 500 mm.
- **Corners:** anticorrosion-coated meshes of 1 cm², 1 m each way, every 500 mm.
- **At 9 points:** both faces get 3–4 mm wire mesh with 300 × 300 mm cells, plus cement plaster.
- **Floors:** timber, beams ≥ 150 × 200 mm at ≤ 1500 mm with double diagonal boarding.
- **Roofs:** light only; **earth-fill roofs are prohibited**.
- **Foundations:** strip foundations required; **unsplit cobblestone is prohibited**.
- **App use:** if the wall material is adobe or earth, apply the Table 3.1 row 6 limits (one storey at 7 points without a frame). Require a timber or RC frame otherwise. Block earth roofs.

### Inferences
- **Key design consequence for private builders.** At 8–9 points, a 2-storey house in **plain** brick needs **category I masonry (Rtu ≥ 180 kPa)**: Table 3.1 row 5 allows 18(4) at 8 and 12(3) at 9. With category II, only 1 storey is allowed, so the house must be built as **complex construction with RC cores**, which allows 22(5) at 8 and 18(4) at 9 even for category II. Since bond is rarely tested on private sites, **the app's safest default for 2+ storeys at ≥ 8 points is "complex construction" with auto-placed cores**.
- **Belt concrete class.** Both KMK and SP set **B12.5** as the minimum, not B15 as the brief assumed. Defaulting to B15 (≈ M200 ready-mix) is a safe and common choice; show "min B12.5 (KMK 3.5.11)".
- **Brick grade.** The KMK minimum for fired brick is **M75**, *lower* than SP 14.13330.2018 (**M100**). Mortar: KMK ≥ M50 vs SP ≥ M25 in summer and M50 in winter. A cautious app could default to M100 brick and M75 mortar while citing the legal minimums.
- **Rule categories for the app.** Hard checks (block): silicate brick, horizontal-void brick, cantilever steps, earth roofs, winter masonry at ≥ 9. Limit checks (warn): storeys/height, transverse-wall spacing, piers, openings, wall area, storey height, h/t. Auto-generate: belts, junction meshes, cores, lintels, foundation layer, parapet cores, partition reinforcement.

### Gaps
- The KMK masonry section does **not** specify belt stirrups, core bar diameters or core section sizes (only "by calculation"). The app needs user defaults, e.g. a common practice of 4Ø10–12 in cores with Ø6 links at 200–250 mm. That figure is **not sourced**; it should be confirmed by an engineer.
- **Chimneys** are not addressed specifically in KMK section 3.5. Only the general non-structural rules apply (SP 6.17.21).
- In Table 3.1 row 4 (complex construction), the column alignment of "ТНСР" for spans and wall step at 7–9 points was read from the text layout. Verify it visually against the PDF before encoding.
- The KMK seismic-joint width (30 mm + 20 mm per 5 m) was read from a fragment of cl. 3.1.3. Re-check the exact wording.

## 4. Russian SP 14.13330.2018 equivalents (for comparison/fallback)

### Takeaway
SP 14.13330.2018 (Minstroy RF order 24.05.2018 No. 309/pr, in force 25.11.2018) carries almost identical detailing rules: belts ≥ 150 mm, B12.5, 4Ø10 / 4Ø12; meshes 1 cm² every 700 / 500 mm; lintels 350 / 250 mm. Its limits differ from the KMK in several places: brick **≥ M100**, transverse walls **18 / 15 / 12 m**, piers **0.64 / 0.9 / 1.16 m** (category I), plain category-I masonry **5 / 4 / 3 storeys** and category II **4 / 3 / 2**. It has no "high" category and no >9 or 9\* levels.

  - Steps are ≤ 0.6 m high at 1:2 (cohesive soil) or 1:3 (non-cohesive).

- **Masonry (cl. 6.14):**
  - 6.14.1: load-bearing walls use mortar with adhesion-increasing additives (plasticisers only are allowed at 7 points). Vertical joints must be filled.
  - 6.14.2: winter masonry banned at ≥ 9 points; at ≤ 8 only with antifreeze additives.
  - 6.14.3: vertical seismic load **15% (7–8) / 30% (9)** of the static vertical load.
  - 6.14.4 units:
    - **brick, ceramic stones and blocks ≥ M100**; vertical voids Ø ≤ 20 mm, square voids ≤ 22 mm, slots ≤ 16 mm; **voidness ≤ 25%** without RC inclusions;
    - limestone or shell-rock ≥ M35; tuff ≥ M50;
    - concrete and aerated blocks for load-bearing walls **≥ B3.5, D600** (self-bearing ≥ B2.5, D500);
    - partitions in brick ≥ M75.
  - 6.14.4 mortar: **≥ M25 in summer, ≥ M50 in winter**; blocks need ≥ M50 or special glue.
  - 6.14.5 categories: **I: Rtu ≥ 180 kPa; II: 120 ≤ Rtu < 180 kPa**; below 120 kPa not allowed. Note: at 7 points natural stone with 60–120 kPa is allowed for **≤ 3 storeys**, piers ≥ 0.9 m, openings ≤ 2 m, wall-axis spacing ≤ 12 m (the KMK says ≤ 2 storeys).
  - 6.14.6: Rt = 0.45·Rtu; Rsq = 0.7·Rtu; Rtw = 0.8·Rtu.

## 5. Eurocode 8 (EN 1998-1:2004 §9) "simple masonry buildings": key numbers for comparison

### Takeaway
EN 1998-1 handles low-rise masonry through **"simple masonry buildings"** (§9.7). These are buildings of importance class I–II that meet geometric rules, meet a **minimum shear-wall area as % of floor area** (Table 9.3), and stay within a storey limit that depends on ag·S. Unreinforced masonry should not be used above **ag·S = 0.20 g** (recommended). Confined masonry requires tie-columns at **≤ 5 m**, at both sides of openings > 1.5 m², and ring beams at every floor (≤ 4 m vertical spacing). Their minimum section is 150 mm, with longitudinal steel ≥ 300 mm² and ≥ 1%.

### Cited Findings
All from [EN 1998-1:2004 (BS EN copy), §9](https://www.phd.eng.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/en.1998.1.2004.pdf). Values are the CEN "recommended values"; national annexes may change them.
- **Units and mortar:**
  - **§9.2.2:** normalised compressive strength of units **fb,min = 5 N/mm²** normal to the bed face and **fbh,min = 2 N/mm²** parallel to the bed face in the wall plane (except low-seismicity cases).
  - **§9.2.3:** mortar **fm,min = 5 N/mm²** for unreinforced or confined masonry and **10 N/mm²** for reinforced masonry.
  - §9.2.4: perpend joints may be fully grouted, ungrouted, or ungrouted with interlock (choice by National Annex).
- **Behaviour factors (§9.3):**
  - Unreinforced masonry designed to EN 1996 alone is recommended only for low seismicity.
  - **Unreinforced masonry satisfying EN 1998-1 may not be used above ag·S = ag,urm, recommended 0.20 g.**
  - Table 9.1 q-factors: URM (EN 1996 only) 1.5; URM (EN 1998-1) 1.5–2.5; **confined 2.0–3.0**; reinforced 2.5–3.0. The lower bounds are recommended.
- **Seismicity thresholds (§3.2.1(4)):** low seismicity is recommended as ag ≤ 0.08 g or ag·S ≤ 0.10 g. Very-low seismicity is ag·S ≤ 0.05 g.
- **Construction rules:**
  - **§9.5.1:** floors and walls connected in two orthogonal directions and vertically, by steel ties or **RC ring beams**; shear walls in ≥ 2 orthogonal directions.
  - **Table 9.2** (minimum effective thickness tef,min / max hef/tef / min l/h). The confined and reinforced rows are legible in the source copy; the unreinforced rows are partly garbled and given as commonly cited:
    - confined masonry: **240 mm / 15 / 0.3**;
    - reinforced masonry: **240 mm / 15 / no restriction**;
    - unreinforced: natural stone 350 mm / 9 / 0.5; other units 240 mm / 12 / 0.4; low-seismicity 170 mm / 15 / 0.35.
  - **§9.5.2 (URM):** horizontal RC beams or steel ties at **every floor level**, vertical spacing **≤ 4 m**, continuous round the periphery. Beam longitudinal steel **≥ 200 mm²**.
- **Confined masonry (§9.5.3):**
  - Confining concrete is **cast after the masonry is built**.
  - Horizontal and vertical confining elements are **≥ 150 mm** in section.
  - **Vertical elements are placed:**
    - at the free edges of each wall;
    - **at both sides of openings > 1.5 m²**;
    - within walls so spacing is **≤ 5 m**;
    - at wall intersections where other confining elements are > 1.5 m away.
  - **Horizontal elements:** at every floor level, vertical spacing ≤ 4 m.
  - **Longitudinal steel ≥ 300 mm² and ≥ 1%** of the element section.
  - **Stirrups ≥ 5 mm at ≤ 150 mm.**
  - Steel class B or C.
  - **Lap splices ≥ 60 bar diameters.**
- **Reinforced masonry (§9.5.4):**
  - Horizontal bed-joint reinforcement at ≤ 600 mm, bars ≥ 4 mm, ≥ 0.05% of the gross section.
  - Vertical reinforcement ≥ 0.08%.
  - ≥ 200 mm² of vertical steel at free edges, at every intersection, and at ≤ 5 m.
  - Parapets and lintels bonded and tied with horizontal reinforcement.
- **Simple masonry buildings (§9.7):**
  - Importance class I–II; no explicit safety verification needed (§9.7.1).
  - **Table 9.3** gives allowed storeys and minimum shear-wall area per direction (% of floor area) for ag·S ≤ 0.07k·g / ≤ 0.10k·g / ≤ 0.15k·g / ≤ 0.20k·g. Several cells in the source copy were OCR-garbled. The legible cells match the commonly cited recommended values below; treat the others as **to be verified**:
    - unreinforced: 1 storey 2.0 / 2.0 / 3.0 / 5.0%; 2 storeys 2.0 / 2.5 / 5.0 / n/a; 3 storeys 3.0 / 5.0 / n/a / n/a; 4 storeys 5.0 / n/a / n/a / n/a;
    - **confined: 2 storeys 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5%; 3 storeys 2.0 / 3.0 / 4.0 / n/a; 4 storeys 4.0 / 5.0 / n/a / n/a; 5 storeys 6.0 / n/a / n/a / n/a**;
    - reinforced: 2 storeys 2.0 / 2.0 / 2.0 / 3.5%; 3 storeys 2.0 / 2.0 / 3.0 / 5.0%; 4 storeys 3.0 / 4.0 / 5.0 / n/a; 5 storeys 4.0 / 5.0 / n/a / n/a.
  - Table 9.3 assumes a minimum unit strength of 12 N/mm² (URM) or 5 N/mm² (confined and reinforced). Correction factor k = 1 + (l_av − 2)/4 ≤ 2 when ≥ 70% of the shear walls are longer than 2 m; otherwise k = 1. The formula text is garbled in the copy; this is the commonly cited form. A roof space above full storeys is not counted.
  - **Plan:** approximately rectangular; **short/long side ratio ≥ λmin = 0.25**; recesses ≤ **15%** of floor area.
  - **Walls:**
    - shear walls almost symmetric in two directions;
    - **≥ 2 parallel walls per direction, each longer than 30% of the building length** in that direction;
    - for at least one direction, the distance between these walls is **> 75% of the building length** in the other direction;
    - **≥ 75% of vertical loads** carried by the shear walls;
    - walls continuous from top to bottom.
  - **Storey-to-storey change:** limits on mass and wall-area change between adjacent storeys (Δm,max, ΔA,max). The values are illegible in the copy; 20% is commonly cited and unverified.
  - **URM:** orthogonal walls connected at **≤ 7 m** spacing.

### Inferences
- **Mapping MSK to ag.** SP 14 uses A = 1.0 / 2.0 / 4.0 m/s² for 7 / 8 / 9 points (≈ 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4 g) ([SP 14.13330.2018 formula 5.2](https://meganorm.ru/Data2/1/4293736/4293736459.pdf)). On that basis:
  - **7 points ≈ ag·S 0.10 g:** EC8 simple confined masonry is allowed up to 4 storeys (wall area 5%).
  - **8 points ≈ 0.20 g:** at the top of Table 9.3, so confined masonry up to 2 storeys (3.5%) and reinforced up to 3 storeys (5%); unreinforced is not allowed above 0.20 g.
  - **9 points ≈ 0.4 g:** beyond the simple-building rules, so explicit analysis is required.
  - This lines up with the KMK/SP approach: at 8–9 points, plain masonry is tightly limited and confined ("complex") construction is the normal solution.
- **EC8 checks worth adding to the app**, because they are easy to compute and missing from the KMK:
  - (a) wall area per direction ≥ pA,min (comparable to KMK Table 3.3: 3 / 4.5 / 6% for category II plain masonry);
  - (b) plan aspect ratio ≥ 0.25 and recesses ≤ 15%;
  - (c) ≥ 2 long parallel walls per direction (> 30% of the building length);
  - (d) tie-columns at both sides of openings > 1.5 m² and at ≤ 5 m spacing. This matches the KMK "pier ends + ≤ 5 m" rule.
- **Confined-column steel check.** EC8 requires ≥ 1% of the column section. A 250 × 250 mm core then needs ≥ 625 mm² (4Ø14 = 616 mm² is marginally short; 4Ø16 = 804 mm² passes). A 150 × 380 mm core needs ≥ 570 mm². The KMK has no such minimum, so this could be shown as an optional "EC8 advisory".

### Gaps
- Some Table 9.2 and 9.3 cells and the Δm and ΔA values were garbled in the only accessible copy. The values above are partly reconstructed and must be checked against an official EN 1998-1:2004 copy.
- I did not establish the status of the second-generation EN 1998 (new parts EN 1998-1-1 / 1998-1-2) and whether its masonry rules changed.

## 6. Common violations in private-house building in Uzbekistan, and what inspectors check

### Takeaway
I found **little direct, sourced evidence** describing specific defects in Uzbek private houses. There are no post-earthquake damage reports for 2020–2024 with detail. Officials and experts do say:
- **rural self-built houses often do not meet seismic norms**, because they are built by owners and unskilled workers;
- **the owner bears responsibility** for construction.

Government inspection campaigns in 2025 focused on multi-storey buildings: unauthorised extra floors, missing permits, low-quality work. The KMK itself defines the controls an inspector can demand: an on-site bond test, stopping work when bond is low, and supervision by state architectural and construction control.

### Cited Findings
- **Private rural houses are the main problem.** "индивидуальное строительство жилых домов в сельских местностях, которые не отвечают нормам по сейсмостойкости". Houses built "самими гражданами и неквалифицированными рабочими" may be damaged or destroyed in strong earthquakes. State-built rural houses do meet the norms. There are no training courses for construction workers, and the ex-UzLITTI test base has been idle since 2010 — [Anhor.uz, 05.03.2019, Sh. Khakimov (ToshuyjoyLITI)](https://anhor.uz/society/seysmostoykoe-stroitelystvo-uspehi-i-problemi-uzbekistana)
- **The owner is responsible.** Deputy Minister of Construction Davron Adilov, after the 2023 Turkey earthquakes: "За строительство объекта отвечает его собственник"; "Я не могу дать гарантию за работу, выполненную 36 млн человек"; "Я гарантирую контроль и стандарты государства" — [Kursiv, 08.02.2023](https://uz.kursiv.media/2023-02-08/v-minstroe-otkazalis-garantirovat-sejsmostojkost-domov-uzbekistana/)
- **2025 inspection results (multi-apartment buildings only):**
  - 9,579 buildings checked for seismic resistance; 8,337 (87%) compliant; 1,242 need instrumental surveys;
  - of 336 surveyed so far: 297 confirmed sound, 71 need strengthening, 1 seismically weak;
  - **201 cases of unauthorised extra floors**, e.g. 21 floors built instead of 14 in Yashnabad, Tashkent;
  - about 200 unauthorised buildings taken to court, 100+ demolished;
  - 2,653 administrative protocols worth about 40 bn sum; 712 low-quality works dismantled; 40 criminal cases;
  - across 242,208 monitoring inspections, 106,905 written orders.
  - The article contains no statements about private houses.

  [Gazeta.uz, 09.09.2025](https://www.gazeta.uz/ru/2025/09/09/violation/)
- **Self-built 1–2 storey houses are a recognised research topic.** Akhmedov & Abirov (2021) assess the stability of "individual self-made one- and two-storey residential buildings" in Uzbek cities and propose strengthening. Only the abstract was accessible — [SciTePress, ISSDRI 2021](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2021/105854)
- **Inspection hooks written into the KMK:**
  - controls on bond strength: mandatory control tests, stop-work when low, strengthening or demolition, removal of the firm after 3 violations (cl. 3.5.4);
  - a mandatory masonry method statement (cl. 3.5.4);
  - a building passport after completion (cl. 6.11);
  - supervision of seismic measures by state architectural and construction control bodies (cl. 6.12).

  [KMK 2.01.03-19](https://eis.su/srostandart/kmk-2-01-03-19/)
- **Grounds for treating construction as illegal:** no land permit or khokim decision, no state expertise, no registration with the Inspection. Reported in a 2025 overview — [Podrobno.uz](https://podrobno.uz/cat/obchestvo/snesti-nelzya-uzakonit-kak-stroitelnyy-bum-v-uzbekistane-ishchet-vykhod-iz-pravovogo-tupika/)

### Inferences
- The following are likely typical violations, derived from KMK requirements that are easy to skip on owner-built sites. They are **not** documented in sources as observed statistics:
  1. No belt at floor or roof level, or a belt narrower or shallower than the wall, or fewer than 4 bars.
  2. No junction meshes, or thin 4 mm wire strips below 1 cm².
  3. Silicate brick or horizontally-perforated brick.
  4. Lean or site-mixed mortar below M50, with no bond test.
  5. Openings wider than 2.5–3.5 m (garage or shop fronts) without an RC frame.
  6. Piers narrower than Table 3.2.
  7. Second storeys on category-II plain masonry (KMK allows only 1 storey at 8–9).
  8. Lintels with short bearing.
  9. Cantilever stairs built into brick.
  10. Heavy earth or adobe roofs.
  11. Rubble or cobble foundations without the reinforced top layer.
  12. Unreinforced parapets or gables over 0.4 m.
- **App use:** build a "site checklist / inspector view" from these items, with a photo field per item:
  - belt before slab, rebar count and diameter;
  - meshes every N courses at junctions;
  - core rebar before concreting;
  - lintel bearing;
  - brick type (no silicate);
  - mortar grade and bond test record;
  - foundation reinforced layer.

### Gaps
- I found no published post-earthquake damage survey of Uzbek private houses for 2020–2024 events, and no official checklist used by inspectors for individual houses.
- The rules for individual housing (permits, typical projects, notification regime) were not researched within the tool budget.
- The June 2025 report that "new buildings must withstand at least 9 points" is not confirmed in the text of Resolution 727.
